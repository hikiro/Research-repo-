# Do and Don't

Practical operating guidance for research and production. Domain-specific rules must be backed by references or project tests and should state their scope.

## Do

- Recover previous decisions, requirements, source files, and working assets before starting a new implementation.
- Inspect the actual production builder, rig, animation methods, and asset provenance instead of assuming a generic helper is used.
- Record the exact source, publication year, URL, relevant passage or figure, and access date for external research.
- Separate what a reference explicitly demonstrates from your interpretation of it.
- Label findings VERIFIED, SOURCE-SUPPORTED, OBSERVED, HYPOTHESIS, or PENDING.
- Describe the character, body plan, size, action, object, environment, camera, and intended effect when recording animation or rigging guidance.
- Study motion in context: preparation, action, contact, resistance, follow-through, recovery, and reaction where relevant.
- Record failed attempts, root causes, fixes, and measurable QA outcomes.
- Preserve before/after comparisons and reproducible test conditions.
- Reuse existing rigs, assets, scripts, templates, and research when they are suitable.
- Test changes on the smallest representative case before expanding to a full production.
- Keep story frameworks conditional on audience, format, pacing, and narrative purpose.

## Don't

- Do not invent sources, measurements, test results, or completed research.
- Do not turn a single example or creator framework into a universal rule.
- Do not assume a humanoid rig, gait, center of mass, or joint arrangement transfers unchanged to another creature or scale.
- Do not change production geometry or animation curves before establishing asset ownership and the source of the defect.
- Do not overwrite protected production files or discard unexplained working-tree changes.
- Do not claim that contact, collision, or visual quality is correct without inspecting the relevant frames or running an appropriate test.
- Do not treat a successful script exit as proof of visual quality.
- Do not hide uncertainty: state what is known, what is inferred, and what remains open.
- Do not silently replace a previous decision; log the reason and evidence for a change.
